# Office Hours diagnostic

## Specific customer
The executor or adult child coordinating a parent's house clear-out while living elsewhere, under a sale or lease deadline. The broader “all families” framing is too vague.

## Painful status quo
Phone photos, family group texts, sticky notes, one hurried estate-sale consult, and a junk-haul deadline. The cost is not only lost resale value: it is sibling conflict, repeat travel, decision fatigue, and irreversible regret.

## Desperate use case
A family has 7–14 days before a home must be empty and cannot tell which 20 of hundreds of items deserve a specialist look. They need a shared exception queue, not an AI appraisal of everything.

## Narrowest paid wedge
A $149 remote “do-not-discard” triage: guided walkthrough, family hold queue, and 20-item specialist-review packet. Do not begin as a marketplace or hauling network.

## Observation plan
Watch five executors conduct an unguided room review. Measure time to first decision, number of reversals, unassigned items, and disagreements. Then concierge-deliver the packet and ask for payment before automating vision.

## Future-fit
More remote families and a larger estate-clearance load make coordination more important. Commodity vision makes object hints cheaper, but trust, provenance, local specialist routing, and a defensible record of consent become the durable layer.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Concierge packet (chosen):** small, transparent, days to deliver; weak automation but strongest learning.
2. **Consumer vision app:** delightful scanning; high false-confidence and acquisition risk.
3. **Estate-operator platform:** stronger B2B retention; longer sales cycle and premature workflow breadth.

**Assignment:** sell three concierge triage sessions before building recognition or marketplace integrations.
